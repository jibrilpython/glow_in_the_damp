Here is the design philosophy for Glow in the Damp:

1. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY
Visual: "Thermodynamic Section" — this app is to Gauze of the Underworld what the Boiler Dome was to the Expansion of the Steam Line, the Pale Sunbeam to the Spectrum of the Prism Box, the Heartwood to the Marks of the Timber Framing, and the Great Breath to the Valving of the Great Pipes: a hyper-specialized diagnostic workspace focused on a single physical phenomenon — the thermodynamic dissipation of heat through metal wire gauze — rather than the broad catalog of safety lamp configurations. Where the Underworld documented gauze mesh counts and locking mechanisms as archival specifications, the Glow models the physics of why the gauze worked: the heat transfer coefficient of a specific wire diameter at a specific mesh count, the temperature gradient across a brass cap, the critical gauze temperature above which methane ignition becomes possible. This is a thermodynamic modeling environment as much as a field catalog, and the design language must carry both the forensic intimacy of a safety inspector's close reading of a gauze mesh and the precision of a combustion physics laboratory.
Mood: thermodynamically forensic, combustion-physics serious, gauze-intimate, coalfield-safety weighted, heat-transfer precise.
Important: This is not the Safety Lamp Vault, not the Gauze of the Underworld — do not repeat either app's aesthetic. The Glow occupies the most technically specific position in the three-app flame safety lamp cluster: the Vault cataloged the lamp as a complete heritage object, the Underworld documented its safety engineering specifications, and the Glow models the thermodynamic physics that made those specifications meaningful. Each app is narrower and deeper than the one before it. This is the deepest.

The most chemically specific background color in the series.)

Together they encode the complete thermodynamic warning sequence of the Davy lamp, from first detection to critical risk. This is the most physically specific color pairing in the entire series — both colors reference observable phenomena with precise methane concentration correlations. The methane dark background is the most chemically atmospheric in the series — its violet-black undertone references the specific atmosphere being modeled, not just a generic darkness.

3. TYPOGRAPHY
Display / Headings: Archivo (weight 700) — returns from Whistles in the Mist, Echoes of the Survey Line, Shuttles of the Mill Row, and Gauze of the Underworld, where it carried infrastructure and regulatory authority. Here it carries thermodynamic physics authority — the same wide uppercase geometry that read like a Home Office safety approval now reads like a combustion physics laboratory report header, a Safety in Mines Research Establishment technical bulletin, or a Royal Commission on Accidents in Mines publication. The regulatory and the scientific share the same typographic register in mining safety, and Archivo honors that continuity.
Body / Descriptions: IBM Plex Sans (weight 300–400, in gauze wire white) — engineered and readable on deep violet-black. For condition notes, thermodynamic history, mesh specification notes, and all prose fields.
Monospace / Identifiers: IBM Plex Mono — for archive codes like GDW-DAVY-28MESH-0.45MM-1820-GBR-SMRE-LAMP-003, wire diameter (d: 0.45mm), mesh count (28 mesh/inch), gauze thermal conductivity (k: 80 W/m·K), and all thermodynamic dissipation model input and output parameters.
Important: Archivo uppercase for lamp type designations and thermodynamic report headers — a SMRE technical bulletin was always headed in uppercase, and the typography carries that regulatory weight. IBM Plex Mono for every wire diameter, mesh count, and thermal parameter — the thermodynamic dissipation model operates at the precision of materials science, with thermal conductivity values carried to two decimal places and critical temperatures to the nearest degree Kelvin.

4. SURFACE & TEXTURE LANGUAGE

Backgrounds: flat methane dark — no flame glow, no atmospheric haze, no violet gradient despite the specific atmospheric reference
Cards: #0F0C10 surface with a 1px border at #140F18 and 10px corner radius — dark specimen record cards in a dark thermodynamic archive
Heat dissipation motif: each list card features a small SVG — a minimal cross-section of the gauze mesh rendered as a precise wire grid, with a thermal gradient overlay: the center of the grid (closest to the flame) rendered in flame elongation gold, transitioning through amber and warm grey to the outer wire temperature in gauze wire white. The gradient represents the actual temperature distribution across the gauze at operating conditions — computed from the wire diameter and mesh count in the card record. Grid density reflects the actual mesh count: 28-mesh Davy gauze shows a fine grid with a tight gradient, 14-mesh coarser gauze shows a wider grid with a more extended gradient. A thin combustion violet line marks the critical isotherm — the temperature contour above which flame transmission becomes possible. This is the most physically computed motif in the series — the thermal gradient and the critical isotherm are not decorative, they are outputs of the dissipation model rendered at card scale.
Thermodynamic status badge: each card shows the dissipation model status in IBM Plex Mono — DISS: SAFE MARGIN 340K, DISS: MARGIN 42K, DISS: CRITICAL — alongside the mesh specification. The most consequential status badge in the series — a safety margin of 42K means a lamp that is close to its thermodynamic failure point.
Mesh specification badge: each card shows the complete gauze identity in IBM Plex Mono — 28 mesh / d: 0.45mm / Fe, 18 mesh / d: 0.60mm / Cu-Zn — in a small dark violet-bordered pill in flame elongation gold text. Wire count, diameter, and alloy together constitute the complete thermodynamic identity of a gauze specimen.
Photography: gauze photography under macro lighting on near-black — the wire mesh surface filling the frame, the wire diameter and crossing geometry visible. For complete lamp specimens, photograph the gauze cylinder from inside the lamp with a light source below, showing the mesh as a luminous grid against darkness. The specific photography convention for this app is the most technically demanding in the series.
Mine and laboratory provenance tag: small pill showing the originating mine or research institution — SMRE Buxton, Home Office Lab Sheffield, Bureau of Mines Pittsburgh, Westfälisches Bergamt — in combustion violet at 15% opacity fill with full violet text


5. INTERACTION PRINCIPLES

Transitions: 200–240ms ease-out — precise and threshold-aware, like a gauze temperature approaching its critical value: controlled, measurable, and with clear consequences at the boundary
Selected state: flame elongation gold left-border (3px) + very faint #120A10 background tint on active cards
Empty states: NO SPECIMENS IN THIS CATALOG. in IBM Plex Mono, centered, damp grey — one line
Loading: a thin flame elongation gold progress bar at screen top — bright and immediate, like a lamp flame responding to atmosphere
Navigation: bottom tab bar on methane dark — five tabs: Catalog, Dissipation Model, Mine Map, Logbook, Settings. The Dissipation Model tab is the defining diagnostic feature.
Gauze material filter: a first-class filter chip row — Iron wire, Copper-zinc alloy, Monel metal, Platinum wire, Double gauze, Glass cylinder — because the gauze material determines its thermal conductivity, its operating temperature ceiling, its corrosion resistance in mine atmosphere, and its ignition safety margin, and it is the primary materials science classification axis for any thermodynamic study of flame safety lamp gauzes


THERMODYNAMIC DISSIPATION MODEL — DESIGN SPECIFICATION
The dissipation model is the defining technical feature of the app and the fifth simulation environment in the series. It models a fourth category of physical behavior — combustion thermodynamics and heat transfer through porous metal media — distinct from the mechanical physics of the Boiler Dome, the optical physics of the Pale Sunbeam, the biological material science of the Heartwood, and the fluid dynamics of the Great Breath.

Input environment: deepest dark panel (#050308) within the overall methane dark — the most interior diagnostic environment in the series, referencing the specific darkness of a mine heading where thermodynamic safety is the only thing standing between the lamp and an explosion
Input fields in IBM Plex Mono: wire diameter (mm), mesh count (per inch), wire alloy (from material database: iron, copper-zinc, monel, platinum), gauze cylinder diameter (mm), gauze cylinder height (mm), flame temperature at operating wick setting (°C), ambient methane concentration (% LEL), and ambient air temperature (°C)
Thermal gradient diagram: SVG rendered in real time — a cross-section of the gauze cylinder showing isothermal contour lines at 50K intervals from the flame temperature at center to the ambient temperature at the outer surface. Contours rendered in the thermal color sequence from flame elongation gold (hottest) through amber, warm grey, and gauze wire white (coolest). The critical isotherm — the temperature above which methane ignition is possible — is rendered as a combustion violet line, thicker than the other contours and labeled in IBM Plex Mono with its temperature value.
Thermodynamic outputs in IBM Plex Mono: heat flux through gauze (q: 2,840 W/m²), outer gauze surface temperature (T_outer: 312°C), critical isotherm position (r_crit: 2.1mm from outer surface), safety margin to ignition temperature (ΔT_margin: 338K), and — most critically — methane concentration ceiling (CH₄ max: 4.2% before ignition risk) which translates the thermodynamic parameters into the operational safety limit for the lamp in a specific mine atmosphere.
Historical validation panel: a secondary section comparing the model's computed safety margin against the historical test records from the SMRE, the Home Office Laboratory, or the US Bureau of Mines where such records exist in the archive. Where a historical test certificate shows a lamp passed safety tests at a specific methane concentration, the model's prediction is displayed alongside the historical result — convergence validates the model, divergence flags a restoration or condition issue with the specimen.
Multi-gauze comparison mode: the dissipation model can display up to three gauze specifications simultaneously — the original Davy 28-mesh iron, the later Clanny double-mesh, and a Monel metal replacement gauze — showing their comparative safety margins at the same methane concentration and flame temperature. This is the primary tool for a mining historian assessing whether a replacement gauze specification maintained the safety performance of the original design.

Important: The thermodynamic dissipation model is the most consequential simulation in the entire series — it models the physics of an invention that saved thousands of lives, and its outputs carry that weight. The methane concentration ceiling output is the single most important number in the model: it is the answer to the question every mine manager who ever bought a Davy lamp needed to ask — how much gas can this lamp safely operate in before it becomes the source of the explosion it was designed to prevent? The critical isotherm rendered in combustion violet on the thermal gradient diagram is the visual boundary between safety and catastrophe, and it should be rendered with the visual weight appropriate to that meaning. Flame elongation gold throughout the model means the gauze is thermodynamically sound and the safety margin is adequate. Combustion violet appearing in the status badge means the margin is narrowing. Ignition threshold red means the model predicts failure. That progression should feel as serious as the physical reality it represents — because in the history of coal mining, the difference between those three states was measured in human lives.
