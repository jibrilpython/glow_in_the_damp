# Glow in the Damp

Description

Glow in the Damp is a technical field catalog and thermodynamic dissipation modeler tailored for industrial mining historians and coalfield heritage curators. This application is dedicated exclusively to the study and preservation of the cooling wire meshes, glass cylinders, and brass caps used in 19th-century flame safety lamps. It focuses entirely on the physics of heat dissipation through metal gauzes to predict safety thresholds in hazardous underground environments.

## Bespoke Object Fields (Model "Flame Safety Lamp"):

•	Gauze Grid Seal: A unique, system-generated colliery database tracking code (e.g., GID-MESH-812-DAVY).

•	Wire Mesh Matrix Density: The precise weave grid of the wire cooling mesh (e.g., 28 wires per linear inch, 784 apertures per square inch).

•	Wire Material Alloy: The physical composition of the cooling mesh wires (e.g., Heavy iron wire, drawn copper wire, early brass wire).

•	Inner/Outer Sleeve Layout: The structural nesting format of the safety gauzes (e.g., Single gauze tube, dual nested concentric sleeves).

•	Air Inlet Ring Pattern: The pathway design through which air enters the combustion chamber (e.g., Bottom-feed horizontal perforations, unbonneted top feed).

•	Glass Cylindrical Grade: The heat-resistance composition of the viewing glass (e.g., High-silica thick-walled glass, clear mica sheet laminate).

•	Reservoir Fuel Lock Class: The tamper-proof locking system used to protect the lamp (e.g., Lead-seal padlock, magnetic internal plunger).

•	Illuminant Fuel Blend: The specific liquid fat or chemical used to fuel the lamp (e.g., Purified colza oil, whale oil, seal fat).

•	Mechanical Housing Dimensions: The total height from the fuel base to the top of the bonnet cap and absolute dry mass in grams.

•	Colliery Shaft Origin: The fictionalized coal basin, deep-vein copper mine, or historical transit tunnel project where the safety lamp was carried (e.g., Black-Seam Shaft No. 4, Iron-Ridge deep drift, Lost Mountain railway tunnel).
